Editable destruction, surface growth and grooming
=================================================
Godot 4.6.1; writable-checkout implementation. No second physics clock, modeling
workspace, application export or patch ZIP is introduced. The repeated physical
debris specification was identical to the already implemented debris/VFX task.

DIRECTABLE DESTRUCTION
----------------------
Use a Basic Shape / Basic Mesh > Properties > Destruction.
- Operations keep the original source resource. Add, disable, duplicate, reset,
  reorder and remove operations through the existing stack. Reset also removes
  that operation's animation keys, with undo restoring them. Explicit depends_on
  references refuse unsafe order/timing rather than silently retargeting geometry.
- Preview cuts shows real prepared cap boundaries as a viewport-only overlay,
  without advancing a future fracture. Operation activation still uses source time.
- Position in viewport moves impact/plane/region centers. Draw fracture path
  places points on the actual mesh; consecutive segments become real cutting
  planes extruded along the view direction. Right-click ends placement. Each point
  is an undoable authored action; paths are limited to 32 segments.
- Weakness regions reduce local physical impact resistance. Protected regions
  reject local automatic impact damage; fracture planes intersecting a protected
  sphere are conservatively skipped. This is deliberately conservative: a plane
  may be refused entirely, not bent around the protected sphere. It is not a
  vertex-painted resistance texture or a guarantee against every manual carve.
- Impact impulse operations apply a timed real impulse/energy event. Fracture
  direction bias mixes authored direction with the normal separation impulse.
  Numeric/vector controls use existing property tracks and curves; topology is
  discrete, never interpolated as mismatched vertex arrays.
- Direct fragments opens a compact editor for individual/multi-selected pieces.
  Pick in the viewport, Shift-click for groups. Drag a gold target to author force
  guidance in one undo action. Root source IDs + prepared geometry identity keep
  selection stable when only timing/impulses/guidance change.
- Fragment controls include initial linear/angular velocity, mass multiplier,
  timed impulse, pin/release time, world target, interval, stiffness and damping.
  Physical, force-guided (contact solved) and explicitly animated-target modes are
  separate. Leaving animated mode carries its last target velocity into physics.
  Targets have standard diamond keyframes; group property edits are atomic.
- Animation-only corrections provide keyed position/rotation offsets, baked
  time offset/rate and freeze-at-scene-time. They do NOT recalculate collisions.
  VFX timing offset is separate. Recalculate with guidance for physical changes.
  Corrections can sample only fragment identities that exist in the sampled bake;
  they do not invent earlier fragments or retime all topology/contacts implicitly.
- Preview, reset/disable stack, recalculate, freeze, bake group and clear-bake
  controls reuse the existing Scene timeline. Late motion edits keep the bounded
  prepared fracture cache; geometry changes produce a new dependent geometry key.
- Capture setup produces a regular Destruction Setup object. Expand Reusable setup
  for normal project Save / Load / Clipboard controls. Apply validates canonical
  source topology/size and copies independent authored settings. It does not blindly
  transfer indexed geometry to a different mesh. Capture baked assembly additionally
  stores a validated complete rigid/destruction group as a reusable scene. Instancing
  remaps identities, transforms and time. Other solvers are explicitly rejected by
  portable-assembly capture rather than claiming unsupported replay.

GROUP BAKES
------------
Bake group records every configured physics actor together, avoiding half-baked
interacting assemblies. Start/end and sampling rate (1-240) are configurable. The
physics fixed-step rate remains independent of output sampling rate. Progress is
shown, cancellation keeps the previous valid bake, and completion is one atomic
undoable action. Closing/changing the scene cancels safely. Existing per-actor
legacy bakes remain readable and their old API remains available.

KiwiPhysicsBakeCache is an ordinary persistent Resource. It interns mesh/material
resources and prepared geometry records once, compresses blocks of 32 samples,
and keeps at most two decoded blocks resident. Snapshots contain rigid fragment
IDs/transforms/velocities, deformable state, topology, structural damage and VFX
event seeds. It does not duplicate a complete rigid mesh at every sample.
The group shares the same immutable cache resource; ordinary actor copies/history
do not clone the whole cache. Copying/moving a world-space assembly invalidates its
old bake as before. Bakes survive scene save/load. Correction layers remain separate.

Limits now include optional external compressed 32-frame chunks, an atomic manifest,
SHA-256 validation, two decoded chunks, 262,144 samples and 4 GiB streamed data.
Embedded expanded data remains bounded at 256 MiB. Prefix reuse validates authored
inputs and resumes on the fixed-step grid; cancellation preserves the prior bake.
Portable rigid/destruction assemblies support timeline retiming. These features do
not imply arbitrary topology editing or new collision solving during baked replay.
Existing contact and convex-source limitations still apply.

SURFACE GROWTH
---------------
In the 3D Model Workspace select a mesh, optionally select faces, then choose
Surface growth... > Hair / Fur / Feathers. The new independent child component
references its source's stable ID. Multiple systems can share one surface.

Actual triangles are sampled proportionally to area using seeded barycentric
coordinates. Roots save IDs, triangle indices, face IDs, barycentric attachment,
orientation and stable variation. Open surfaces are supported. Selected polygon
faces on a native bound mesh are mapped to corresponding weighted triangles.
Density reserves candidate roots (normally 2x visible density) for local density
painting. Minimum spacing uses a spatial grid. Ordered rows project a lattice onto
the actual surface; this is useful for locally planar wings, not a general curved
UV row-unwrapping system. Appearance/length edits do not reshuffle roots.

Hair/fur use batched tapered ribbons with straight/curved/wavy/coiled shapes,
length/thickness/direction variation, bending and optional fur guard strands.
Feathers add distinct vanes and raised central shafts, with down/contour/flight
profiles, width and overlap controls. They are real geometry, not a fur texture.
Fine barb geometry and specialized anisotropic hair shading are not supplied.

Components have source, density, length, thickness, seed, quality, shape, taper,
variation, minimum spacing, direction (normal/world/local/tangent), color and
feather-specific settings. Regenerate and convert-to-editable-mesh are supported.
Rename, hide, duplicate, parent hierarchy and native model save/load use existing
component systems. Source deletion is refused while dependent growth remains.
Source topology changes invalidate/hide old attachments and request explicit
regeneration; they never silently reuse unrelated triangle indices. Compatible
vertex deformations retain barycentric roots. GPU shader displacement and arbitrary
runtime blend-shape evaluation are not sampled by the attachment adapter.

Skin attachment evaluates the actual weighted source vertices, then barycentrically
evaluates each root and its deformed frame. This avoids treating a blended triangle
as one rigid bone. Rigid bone attachments are handled separately. Cached styles
are deformed at pose time; root distribution is not regenerated for bone motion.
Scene instantiation retains procedural component data and resolves the instance's
own skeleton. Repeated paused poses reuse geometry. Native model resources and
the existing model Super Clipboard save retain dependencies; a standalone growth
component without its source is not a self-contained asset.

GROOMING
----------
Select a growth component and enable Groom in viewport. The contextual inspector
offers Brush, Comb, Cut, Grow, Curl, Clump, Density, Length, Smooth, Relax, Part,
Feather width, Spread, Protect, Reset region and Guide, each with a distinct icon.
Shift-wheel changes world brush radius. Ctrl inverts applicable tools. Smooth
falloff, spacing, target-versus-add modes, local-axis symmetry and through-surface
editing are supported. Posed surface hits are mapped back into rest coordinates.
Default face/normal filtering avoids the opposite facing side; this is not an
exact geodesic brush or a separate occlusion ray for every neighboring strand.

Tools change actual curves/lengths/density/feather geometry. Roots are fixed.
Density reveals/suppresses stable reserved candidates; it cannot exceed the reserved
root pool without increasing the base density/regenerating. Curl preserves sampled
arc length approximately. Feathers bend rather than receiving automatic spiral
curls. Clumps retain root-specific attachments and saved cell/target relationships.
Smooth averages local direction/offset/length/density; Relax reduces deviations.

One mouse stroke becomes one sparse ordered grooming layer and one undo action.
Escape cancels. Enable/disable, reorder/remove, reset and apply layers are available.
Layer changes store per-root field targets; ordering is deterministic last-writer
composition, not an arbitrary replayable boolean geometry stack. The source mesh
is unchanged. Applying layers consolidates targets into the base style; undo restores
the layers. Limits: 128 layers / 65,536 sparse edit entries before consolidation.

Guide mode: click a root to create a major guide affecting nearby roots. Gold
control points can be dragged in 3D; the root cannot move. The inspector switches
guides, changes influence, adds/removes tip points, smooths and duplicates/removes
guides. Point drags use nearest-handle selection and one undo entry. Guides express
root-relative style; this is not a full multi-guide interpolation/lock constraint
solver. Independent per-strand numeric selection, arbitrary cutting planes, brush
presets, continuous guide rotation gizmos and advanced clump scheduling remain
limited. No strand physics or separate grooming workspace is added.

PERFORMANCE AND QUALITY
-----------------------
20,000 candidate roots per component; one million generated vertices; 100,000
source vertices/faces. Ordered rows have a two-million triangle-query ceiling.
Fast/Balanced/Detailed use 3/6/10 curve segments. Limits reject excessive requests
with visible feedback rather than allocating unbounded strand nodes.

One MeshInstance per growth component, not one per strand. Spatially ordered chunks
of 128 roots cache unchanged geometry. Brush queries use a root spatial grid.
Local edits replace only affected ArrayMesh surfaces. Dense built-in materials now
deform strands on the GPU using source attachment and weighted guide-pose textures.
Source skinning uploads source vertices; each instance owns its pose tables. Small
grooms and arbitrary custom shaders retain CPU deformation. Distance LOD compacts
geometry and final-quality scene-video capture restores full coverage. See
growth_rendering_progress.txt for current measurements and limits.

Measured on this machine: 8,000 reserved roots, radius .15, four local comb stamps
affected 115-140 roots and rebuilt 6 of 63 chunks. Full brush + geometry update was
about 32-44 ms per stamp in the final measured run. This is not a 60 FPS guarantee.
Initial 8,640-root generation/rendering was hundreds of milliseconds depending on
load; changing a small region avoids repeating that entire initial generation.
The 31-sample two-actor destruction fixture used about 1.2 KiB compressed state
plus three interned resources / one prepared geometry record (simple fixture only).
