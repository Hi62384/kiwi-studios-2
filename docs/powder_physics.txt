POWDER / GRANULAR SOURCES, PHYSICS AND PHYSICAL PROPERTIES
=======================================================

Getting started
---------------
Scene Workspace > Add actor > Simulation > Powder.
The normal Actor tab contains transforms, parenting and timeline Physics controls.
Properties contains Powder, Source, Physical Properties and Preview sections.

Powder: enable the actor, select Finite volume / Emitter / Volume + emitter,
choose spacing, packing jitter and a saved seed. Simulate grains is on by default.
Turn it off to preview source creation without integrating grain motion.

For a first pile: add a wide, thin Basic Shape box as a floor, enable its Physics
and choose Static collider. Place Powder above it and press Play. Visual geometry
without Physics enabled is not automatically a collider. To pour, choose Emitter
and set a finite supply or emission end time. Use lower grain counts while editing,
then Bake for repeatable playback/rendering of more expensive scenes.

Source: choose Box, Sphere/ellipsoid, Cylinder or Closed mesh actor. A mesh source
uses the actual source actor's transformed, indexed triangles, including compatible
multiple surfaces, child meshes and sampled skin geometry. It does not fill the
bounding box. The source mesh is never modified. Point, Disk, Box and Sphere
emitters have rate, start/end source times, direction, speed, initial velocity,
spread, radius/dimensions and continuous/finite supply controls. Emission rate and
other emitter controls can be animated. The total preview/simulation budget always
applies, including when continuous supply is enabled.

The initial finite volume is generated once at activation. Editing its structural
settings invalidates/reconstructs it, but ordinary timeline playback does not
reposition released grains when a parent moves. Emission samples the source's
world transform at each recorded birth time. Actor > Physics provides actor/bone
attachment, offset and optional inherited source velocity.

Source errors are shown in the Powder status and physics status: missing source,
open/nonmanifold/degenerate/inconsistently wound mesh, invalid dimensions, impossible
grain spacing, empty sampling, excessive sampling cells or grain count. Over-budget
finite volumes return no points, never an unexplained partial fill. Increase
spacing, reduce the source size or raise Maximum grains. Mesh sampling requires
closed, non-self-intersecting shells; robust self-intersection repair is not supplied.

The source generator, dedicated granular solver and MultiMesh renderer are separate.
Grains use packed positions/velocities, IDs, radii, masses, birth positions/times,
source tags, contact counts, compaction and resting state. Cohesive bonds are also
checkpointed. There is one instanced draw, not a Scene actor/node/body per grain.
Only reusable sphere-query probes and actor/proxy collision geometry are registered
with the native collision query world. Selected source guides are editor overlays
and do not appear in scene-video rendering.

Physical Properties
-------------------
Grain radius (metres): actual sphere contact radius and rendered size. Density
(kg/m³) gives mass = density * 4/3 * pi * radius³. Seeded radius variation is in
Advanced. Source spacing must accommodate the largest grain, independently of the
preview mesh's tessellation. Defaults are deliberately coarse, visible spheres;
this is not a microscopic dust solver or a claim to simulate millions of real grains.

Friction: Simple-mode base coefficient, combined geometrically with the other
surface/grain. Flowability derives static friction, sliding friction and rough-grain
rolling resistance from this base. Increasing Flowability reduces resistance;
it never writes over the authored friction or cohesion values during simulation.

Advanced contact coefficients: copies the current effective Simple coefficients
when enabled, as one undoable edit, then uses explicit static/dynamic/rolling values.
Basic Friction and Flowability are hidden while Advanced mode is active. Returning
to Simple mode derives a matching base static coefficient; an independently edited
advanced dynamic/static ratio cannot be represented exactly by the Simple model.

Cohesion (N/contact): actual tensile contacts/bonds, formed near touching grains,
limited by tensile force and broken past the configured strain. It is not just drag.
Compaction resistance changes compliant contact stiffness, with a hard penetration
safety limit. Packing/contact history can strengthen bonds slightly. Grains rearrange
and never shrink their stored radii to fake compression.

Bounciness controls normal restitution, including grain/grain and surface impacts.
Damping is exponential free-motion energy loss (1/s). Contact damping removes
residual closing velocity. Wind influence multiplies aerodynamic response based on
relative airflow, exposed area, density/mass and approximate contact sheltering.
Small/light grains respond more strongly. Actor > Physics > Wind is airflow velocity
in m/s; optional turbulence is seeded/time-driven, never wall-clock random.
Existing gravity, source velocity, force and impulse events are used. Gravity alone
does not accelerate denser grains faster in vacuum.

Advanced also exposes gravity influence, bond breaking strain, air drag, fluid
interaction strength, quality, substeps, iterations, safety speed, rest thresholds
and optional bone/deformable collision proxies. All are connected to calculations.

Animation and editing
---------------------
Density, friction/flowability (or Advanced coefficients), cohesion, restitution,
damping, compaction resistance, wind, gravity influence, turbulence, fluid strength
and supported source/appearance values use ordinary actor keyframes and curves.

Radius/size distribution, volume shape/dimensions, spacing, seed, source mode,
budget and base solver counts are structural, deliberately non-keyframable. Editing
them regenerates/replays the source instead of interpolating grain counts or
rebuilding the population every frame. Transform animation remains available.

Standard inspector gesture handling gives one undo per slider/number drag and
supports common-property multi-edit. Physical changes invalidate later checkpoints;
color/roughness/source-guide changes reuse the physical cache. Normal scene saves
store authored settings, tracks and references, not duplicate generated populations.
Bakes and explicit Freeze store full packed state. Mesh/attachment and bond references
are remapped when related actors are copied or scenes instantiated.

Solver and supported interactions
---------------------------------
The CPU solver uses spatial hashing, pair deduplication, unilateral compliant
position-based sphere constraints (XPBD), Coulomb static/dynamic positional friction,
rough-grain tangential resistance, restitution and breakable tensile bonds. Adaptive
substeps and swept native boundary queries limit tunnelling. Overlapping initial
births are repaired without converting the repair into explosive stored energy.
Resting suppresses residual drift, not gravity/support checks: removed supports,
moving geometry, new contacts, forces and wind can wake the grains.

Static, animated and dynamic rigid geometry uses the existing offline native query
space, including static concave triangle colliders. The space is never continuously
stepped by the engine. Moving surfaces supply linear/angular contact velocity.
Dynamic bodies receive collected contact impulses/torques and positional response
using an aggregate reduced-mass/stabilized approximation, rather than one independent
infinite-mass impulse per grain. A confined pile supports a falling test block.
The native test also increases that block from 2 kg to 20 kg: the bed compacts,
the block remains supported, and grain radii are unchanged.

Different Powder actors share the same contact solve, respecting layers/masks.
Soft/cloth/rope simulation points have sphere proxies and receive point reactions.
Animated skeletons use bone-origin sphere proxies with one-way response. These are
approximate, discrete proxies, NOT sealed cloth triangles, full bone capsules,
two-way animated-character dynamics, or complete watertight deformable coupling.

Existing Fluid particles exchange kernel-sampled drag, displaced-volume buoyancy,
flow velocity and mass-weighted contact/exclusion with grains. Fluid gets opposite
drag/buoyancy momentum and contact displacement, not decorative splashes. Solid
boundaries reproject coupling corrections. The liquid pressure solver stays separate.
Frozen/baked actors act as prescribed/infinite-reservoir states, not a second live
solver. Equal playback speeds are recommended for physically interpretable coupling.

Timeline, persistence and limits
-------------------------------
All generation and integration is on KiwiTimelinePhysics's fixed scene-time clock.
Pause performs no hidden stepping. Seek restores checkpoints and replays. IDs,
velocities, emission fraction, bonds, contact/packing/rest state are restored.
Clip trims/source offsets, playback speed and activation use the same timeline path.
Fractional display interpolation respects births and activation/reset discontinuities.

Actor > Physics offers Bake, Clear cache, Recalculate and Freeze. Baked playback and
scene-video rendering use the same sampled state; rendering speed/frame rate does
not advance a separate simulation. Bakes are capped at 4,096 fixed frames / 64 MiB
per actor, checked during collection. Checkpoint estimates include cohesive bonds.

Limits: sources at most 65,536 grains / 200,000 candidate cells / 100,000 triangles;
contact broadphase at most 1,000,000 candidate pairs and 65,536 bonds per actor.
Budget errors/warnings preserve grain identities; no silent particle thinning.
CPU simulation above 4,096 grains produces an explicit performance warning. This
is an interactive small-scene/coarse-grain foundation; large dense scenes should
use lower resolution or baking. No GPU granular solver is included. Native collision
queries and CPU contact work would still require synchronization/readback with a
GPU path; the tested CPU path was preferred over a disconnected GPU visual effect.

Other limitations: spherical grains have no independent orientation/spin/shape mesh
collision; rolling is an effective resistance, not true nonspherical tumbling.
Rigid feedback uses approximate inertia and stabilized aggregate impulses, not a
monolithic rigid/grain constraint solve. Contacts have finite timestep/iteration
accuracy. Fluid coupling is particle-resolution dependent and approximate, with
safety velocity caps; it is not high-fidelity porous-flow/erosion/wetness simulation.
No chemical mixing, absorption, mud or advanced continuous bulk-surface rendering
is claimed. Grain-resolution deposition/transport and reusable materials are now
described in powder_materials_and_destruction.txt. Extremely small grains remain
limited by native collision skin/precision.

Verification (Godot 4.6.1, Windows, Ryzen 7 6800H / RTX 3060 Laptop)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
New scripts: powder_source_test, powder_timeline_test, powder_solver_test,
powder_fluid_test, powder_physics_test, powder_system_test, powder_workspace_test.
They cover geometric interiors (including a torus hole), mesh errors, all source
timing/seed/replay, copying/instancing, real floor and moving geometry contacts,
piles, slope/friction/flow differences, cohesion/breakage, compaction, density/wind,
restitution, support removal, rigid support, two-way fluid momentum/trajectories,
parent/bone sources, cache invalidation/undo, scene round trips, native inspector
gestures, batched rendering, freezing/baking and two temporary scene-video runs.
Temporary test video files are removed; no application export is produced.

Historical source/solver measurements before the material/transport extension
(not guaranteed real-time rates; variant rendering has additional CPU cost):
- 27,000-grain box generation: about 31-40 ms; CPU preview-buffer upload ~8.5 ms
  in the native batched-preview check (not a complete GPU frame-time measurement).
- 512 grains, 2 substeps / 6 iterations, free-space neighborhood benchmark:
  about 40-60 ms per fixed step (no native-boundary or liquid-rendering costs).
- 64-grain analytical-floor pile: ~0.35 m high, ~0.0022 m maximum pair overlap;
  settled one-second drift below 0.0001 m in the recorded checks.
- Native emitter test: 36 grains pile up and remain near rest after emission ends.
- Sand/water test: coupled grains descend more slowly and alter liquid trajectories;
  shared replay exactly reproduces the recorded grain and liquid arrays.

Machine-readable results and screenshots are in .godot/verification/. See the
powder_acceptance, powder_regressions and final edge-case reports for the actual
tested revision/results. Existing Fluid/rigid/soft/cloth/rope, timeline, inspector,
scene saving and viewport interaction regressions are run alongside the new scripts.
At delivery, 20 distinct regression scripts passed, plus additional repeats for
edge cases and two successful OpenGL-compatibility runs. The seven powder scripts
contain 114 successful checks in their latest versions. Native Scene launches and
temporary scene-video encoding were exercised; no application export was built.
The broad actor_types_test passes its assertions but still emits the previously
observed SkyShaderRD/texture/resource shutdown warnings; targeted powder runs do
not emit those warnings. The Windows test environment also prints an unrelated
root-certificate-store warning during engine startup.
