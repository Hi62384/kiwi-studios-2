KIWI TIMELINE PHYSICS
====================

Getting started
---------------
Select a 3D actor, then open Actor > Physics and turn Physics on.
The existing scene playhead controls every solver. Nothing advances while paused.
No separate physics timeline, Engine.time_scale changes, or live rigid/soft body
nodes are used.

Falling objects: choose Rigid Body. For the floor, enable physics on a box or mesh
and set Motion to Static collider. Extend the floor's clip across the simulation.
An Animated collider follows its ordinary transform keys. Collision only hides
the geometry without deleting its mesh or changing its saved visibility.

Flexible objects: choose Soft Body or Cloth on a triangle mesh. For basic shapes,
add subdivisions in Properties first. Stretch, bend, damping, wind and recovery
control the deformation. Use an edge pin region, or expand Point pins and enable
Pick pins in viewport. Click a simulation point to pin it; Ctrl-click removes a
pin and Esc finishes picking. Pins can stay in world space or follow the chosen
actor/bone attachment. The numeric point picker is also available.

Rope: choose Rope on a Point 3D or mesh actor. Set length, visible radius and point
count. Attach either or both ends to actors/bones, or leave them free. Explicit
point pins can hold intermediate rope points. The source mesh supplies the rope
material; a Point 3D uses the default tube appearance.

Object secondary motion: Ragdoll / Jiggle follows an animated target with spring
lag, or uses articulated Fixed, Hinge, Slider, Distance or Spring constraints.
Attachment offset is in attachment space. Body pivot is local to the moving body.
Hinge/slider axes are in the animated attachment's orientation.

Skeletons: select the imported Skeleton Rig actor, choose Skeleton Ragdoll /
Jiggle and check the affected bones. Other actor types offer shortcuts to select
an available rig. Search, All, None, Selected, Children, Chain and Mirror help
choose bones. Per-bone settings apply to highlighted tree rows, or to all checked
bones if none are highlighted. Unchecked bones keep their animated local poses;
they naturally inherit movement from a simulated ancestor. Jiggle springs toward
the animation. Ragdoll releases the checked bones within their joint-angle limits.
A checked root can fall in full-body ragdoll; partial chains stay attached to
their animated parents. Influence blends the rendered pose with animation.

Controls and animation
----------------------
Activation and Stop are SOURCE seconds, matching clip-local property keyframes.
Start times, source offsets and clip speed participate in scene-time evaluation.
Trimming into a clip replays the earlier source history instead of restarting it.
Enable and scalar/vector controls expose ordinary keyframe diamonds. Animated
targets are sampled at each fixed step. Initial velocity can inherit parent motion
at release. Forces and impulses are undoable timed events in source seconds.

The Advanced fixed-step setting requests 30-240 steps per scene second. All
interacting actors share the highest requested rate; the default is 60. Clip speed
scales that actor's source-time integration. Display frames interpolate between
fixed samples and need not match the solver rate.

Animated uses ordinary actor animation. Simulated integrates physics. Guided adds
a spring toward the animated target (deformables use their pins/attachments).
Freeze captures the current world-space transform, points and bone poses. Choosing
Frozen in Control performs the same capture. Bake records a scene-time interval;
Baked becomes available after a successful bake. Recalculate returns a baked actor
to its source mode and replays it. Clear cache discards transient checkpoints but
does not delete saved bake data.

Undo/redo applies to authored configuration, bone selection, attachments, pins,
events, freeze and bake. Simulation steps never enter the edit history. A numeric
drag previews continuously and commits as one undo action.

Saving and rendering
--------------------
Actor.values.physics is saved by the existing native scene resource system.
It includes configuration, stable actor IDs, bone names, overrides, pins, events,
frozen states and baked frames. Copy/instantiate operations remap attachment IDs.
Transient checkpoint dictionaries and native physics RIDs are not saved.

Bake validity fingerprints relevant transforms, geometry, clip timing, driving
animation, attachments, physics settings and curve data. Actor names, selection
and unrelated appearance changes do not invalidate caches. A stale bake produces
a visible warning and live replay; it is never silently reused. Referenced actor
and bone deletions also produce warnings. Editing mesh topology may require
re-pinning simulation points, whose indices refer to the coarse simulation mesh.

Video rendering waits for the same scene controller to finish a requested seek
before capturing a frame. It does not advance physics by render duration. Native
tests render the same rigid/baked-cloth scene twice and compare each sampled state
with viewport seeking. This is a scene-video test, not an application export.

Architecture and bounds
-----------------------
KiwiTimelinePhysics owns fixed-step states and periodic checkpoints. Each snapshot
includes velocities, activation, sleep data, point arrays, bone endpoints and root
motion. Backward seeks restore a prior checkpoint and replay. A 12 ms scheduling
budget yields between steps during longer seeks, without changing the solver's
mathematical timestep. Playback holds its requested time while catching up.

PhysicsServer3D supplies native Jolt/Godot swept collision queries in an INACTIVE,
isolated space. It never ticks with the game's real-time physics clock. Godot 4.6
uses instant-updated static RIDs here strictly as query proxies: kinematic RID
transforms would wait for an engine tick even in an inactive space. Actor motion
modes and velocities are entirely controlled by Kiwi, not those native RID modes.
Godot 4.6
does not expose safe public per-world manual stepping/restoration for ordinary
RigidBody3D, SoftBody3D, PhysicalBone or SpringBone simulation, so their live clocks
are not used. Integration and constraints are implemented by this controller.

Soft body/cloth meshes transfer coarse-point displacements to triangle vertices,
preserve UVs and materials, and rebuild normals. Skinned cloth initializes using
CPU skinning at its activation pose; disabling physics restores the original mesh
and skin. Rope renders an eight-sided 3D tube. Bone physics runs after ordinary
animation and before final skin/rigid-bone attachments are displayed.

Limits: 256 soft/cloth points; 96 rope points; 30,000 render vertices per deforming
actor; 96 checkpoints within an estimated 64 MiB budget. A bake is limited to 4,096
fixed-step frames and 64 MiB per actor. Use shorter intervals or lower simulation
resolution for larger scenes. Geometry is captured at activation, not continuously
re-topologized while physics runs; animated actor scale and transforms remain
supported. Static triangle collision is static-only; moving concave requests use
a warning and a box fallback. An actor must have nonzero scale on every axis.

Important solver limitations
----------------------------
This is a functional timeline-oriented constraint system, not the complete native
Jolt dynamics engine. Rigid contacts use swept motion with bounded approximate
isotropic angular response, not full inertia tensors/manifolds, robust vehicle
dynamics or production-quality stacks of hundreds of bodies. Articulated joints
are projected constraints, not native Jolt joints.

Skeleton contacts use bone-tip spheres and angular constraints, not automatically
fitted full anatomical collision capsules. Soft bodies use elastic distance/bend
constraints and shape recovery, not volumetric finite-element physics. Cloth and
rope collide against enabled rigid colliders; they do NOT push those rigid bodies
back, collide with one another, or support self-collision, true knotting, frictional
wrapping or automatic clothing/dressing. Those features need additional solver
work. Physics influence blends the display; use Animated mode when a rigid body
should instead act as an animation-controlled collider.

Replay is tested with Godot 4.6.1/Jolt on the same machine. Bit-exact unbaked replay
across different engines, physics backends or hardware is not promised. Keep saved
bakes for important renders. Very dense/fast or ill-conditioned scenes may need
more fixed steps, more constraint iterations and simpler collision geometry.
