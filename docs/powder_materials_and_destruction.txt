Powder materials, transport and destructible geometry
===================================================
Godot 4.6.1. Extends the existing actor registry, property inspector, modeling
topology, timeline physics, offline query space, resource picker and clipboard.
No separate physics timeline or material workspace. No application export.

Powder workflow
---------------
Add actor > Simulation > Powder. Properties > Appearance has seven illustrated
recipes: Sand, Snow, Dirt, Gravel, Dust, Salt and Sugar. Choose a recipe, choose
Both / Appearance only / Physics only, then click the checkmark to apply.
One application, including changes to spacing required by larger radii, is one
undo action. Source dimensions, parenting and clip timing are not overwritten.

Rounded, angular, irregular and crystal representations use cached geometry
variants. Custom mode accepts up to three Mesh resources. Detail is manually
selectable (low/distant, balanced, close-up); there is not automatic distance LOD.
Seeded IDs retain mesh variant, orientation and surface random values in motion.
These are visual representations: collisions remain spherical, and independent
physical grain rotation/angular momentum is not solved.

Colors, roughness variation, metallic/specular response, opacity, local UV
scale/rotation/offset, texture blend/tint, procedural mineral detail and normal
strength are editable/animatable. Albedo, normal and roughness slots support
images and procedural Noise Images through the usual project/clipboard picker.
Standard 3D, spatial Shader Material and Natural Material overrides reuse the
existing adapters. Overrides replace built-in surface shading; their own shader
must consume instance variation if desired. Canvas/sky/fog shaders are rejected
for grain surfaces. There is one shared surface per batch, not one per grain.

Expand the Powder Material resource to edit it; right-click the slot for Save As,
Load, Make Unique, Clear or Super Clipboard. Its name is editable. Saved assets
share appearance; embedded recipes/Make Unique are independent. Built-ins are
fresh recipes, never global mutable preset resources. Clearing a reference does
not delete a project file; use the existing filesystem delete action for that.
The ellipsis menu can capture current settings, restore a built-in, adopt saved
physics defaults, reactivate deposits at the playhead, or save attached grains as
an ordinary mesh resource.

The material's physics values are RECIPE DEFAULTS. The actor's existing physical
properties remain the simulation authority. Adopt saved physics explicitly to
copy new defaults into the actor. Appearance linkage may be disabled to customize
local visual controls; unlink copies the current appearance so it does not jump.
Appearance edits/keyframes, including nested saved-resource properties, do not
invalidate trajectories. Adopted radius, density, friction, etc. do invalidate.

Transport workflow
------------------
Properties > Deposition & Transport: enable deposition. Other controls adjust
adhesion, settling speed, attached coating limit, compaction, collision release,
detachment exposure, wind/fluid erosion and airborne classification. Existing
density/friction/cohesion, aerodynamic drag, turbulence and wind are reused.
The source-time Reactivate field releases deposits without deleting their mass.
Physics > Powder surface on a supporting object controls accumulation, adhesion
and maximum attached coating. Freeze, Recalculate and Bake are the existing
physics actions. All effects stop on pause and replay from timeline checkpoints.

Settled grains retain packed IDs, radii, masses and surface-local attachment
records. Integration and moving constraints skip attached grains. Sparse surface
cells derive coverage, thickness and mass. They follow rigid/animated supports;
bone/deformable collision proxies provide approximate attachments. Moving solid
queries release displaced grains. Wind and fluid shear overcome weight/friction,
cohesion, adhesion and compaction over time. Released grains re-enter the existing
granular/fluid coupling and may settle again. Airborne classification refers to
the same physical grains, not duplicated decorative particles.

Limits: this is a grain-resolution hybrid, not a continuous high-resolution
surface coating or a compressed microscopic-material solver. Static deposits
still participate in the spatial contact hash and retain per-grain inventory.
There is no volumetric dust renderer, erosion of underlying mesh topology,
porous flow, chemical mixing, absorption or mud model. Very fine dust needs an
appropriate artistic simulation scale because native collision precision and
CPU particle budgets are finite. Supporting objects need compatible colliders.

Destruction workflow
--------------------
Select an ordinary Basic Shape or Basic Mesh, Properties > Destruction. Enable
it, choose Manual / Physics / Combined, and add operations. Manual editing does
not require enabling a separate dynamic rigid-body toggle. Operations have stable
IDs, editable timing, enable/remove/reorder controls and contextual parameters.
Float/vector parameters have normal keyframe buttons. Position in viewport (or
Damage D in the viewport toolbar) exposes a draggable center, with Ctrl snapping
and Escape cancellation. Each drag is one undo action even after actor selection
changes. The source mesh remains intact; operations and bake data save in scenes.
Numeric/vector drags preview immediately. Keyed controls (including viewport
radius scrolling) insert a keyframe rather than overwriting the base value.
Removing an operation removes its animation tracks in the same undo action;
undo restores both, so old tracks cannot resurrect a deleted operation.

Supported real topology/geometry operations:
- Plane split with capped interiors.
- Seeded multi-plane and impact-biased fracture into independent convex pieces.
- A narrow geometric crack/crevice made by clipping out a channel.
- Curved vertex bending, localized dents, twisting and stretching.
- Plane tearing of open/thin sheets into independently moving regions.
- Sphere/polyhedral, box and cylindrical carving using convex half-space cuts.

Cuts preserve exterior UV/material assignments and generate interior UVs/normals/
tangents. An interior color or compatible material can be supplied. Fragments have
stable identities, individual world transforms, inherited motion, volume-weighted
mass and convex query colliders. The original unbroken collider is disabled.
Carved pieces use compound convex colliders; static concave deformed surfaces can
use triangle collision, while dynamic concavity uses an explicit convex proxy.
Save evaluated mesh creates a normal reusable mesh at the playhead without
overwriting source geometry. Geometry conversion is capped at 150,000 vertices;
grain custom-instance shading may need adaptation on a standalone snapshot mesh.

Limits: solid cuts/fractures currently require CLOSED CONVEX components. Model
components imported as separate scene mesh actors can be edited independently.
Open sheets support plane tears, not arbitrary freehand tear propagation or
strain-driven rebuilding of a cloth graph. Skinned/rigged topology changes and
non-white vertex-colored inputs are explicitly rejected with the source intact;
make a static posed/textured copy first. There is no arbitrary concave Boolean,
arbitrary cutting mesh, drawn crack-path graph, branching crack solver, FEM or
structural-collapse engine. Bending/dents are geometric approximations. Impact
triggers use available rigid-contact energy, not a solved continuum stress field.
Fragment motion uses the shared query world with approximate rigid response,
not exact compound inertia or fully coupled fragment/grain momentum exchange.

Material-dependent damage
-------------------------
The Destruction Material slot/preset selector provides Glass, Steel, Aluminum,
Wood, Stone, Concrete, Plastic, Ceramic and Rubber. These are editable illustrative
artistic energy recipes, not experimentally calibrated engineering materials.
Strength/toughness change failure thresholds; hardness and bendability change dent
depth and yielding; brittleness favors cracking/shattering instead of ductile
deformation; density can optionally derive mass from source volume. Directional
weakness, branching/count bias, irregularity, fragment size, seed, fatigue and rate
sensitivity affect actual response and split planes. Explicit advanced yield/
fracture energies, stiffness and tensile/compressive/shear coefficients remain
authoritative when enabled; basic controls do not overwrite them each step.

Damage is recorded in local spatial cells, so small repeated impacts accumulate
locally. Actor-level physical materials, separate imported component actors and
two explicit spherical local material regions are supported. Regional assignment
overrides actor assignment; an explicit physical association on a visual material
is the fallback. Interior/exterior visual associations remain separate from
physical coefficients. Save/load/Make Unique/Super Clipboard use ordinary objects.
Manual Geometry Editing bypasses physical resistance unless Physical resistance
is enabled on an operation; its energy then limits cutting/deformation.

Limits: regions select local response coefficients; fracture still operates on a
compatible component, not a fully bonded heterogeneous solid. There is no face-
painting assignment UI, inter-material connection-strength solver, realistic wood
fiber model, spiderweb crack propagation, rough non-planar cut interiors or tensile
stress solver. 'Branching' is a deterministic fragmentation/count/direction bias,
not an explicit branching fracture network. Elastic recovery is a geometric
response model. Physics damage events currently originate in rigid contact motion.

Performance and verification
----------------------------
At most eight grain geometry batches (one per geometry variant), shared materials,
cached geometry, stable custom instance data, no actor/node per grain. Destruction
limits: 20,000 source vertices, 48 pieces, up to 16 requested pieces per fracture,
24 cached preparations and a 32 MiB preparation-geometry budget. Existing physics
checkpoint/bake limits apply. Geometry is reused when operations are unchanged.
Large sources, many contacts and complex cutting remain CPU-heavy; use baking.

Regression reports/logs and native screenshots are in .godot/verification/.
New tests cover recipes, geometry/UVs, stable grain identity, image dependencies,
saved/shared/unique resources, clipboard, appearance keyframes/cache behavior,
surface deposition/following, wind/fluid detachment, foot and rolling-cylinder
displacement, exact inventory/replay/bakes, real closed cuts, carving collision
holes, independent fragments, material-dependent drops, local damage, inspector
buttons and viewport undo/redo. Existing Powder, Fluid, rigid/soft/cloth/rope,
timeline, model geometry/import, scene serialization, inspector and shader tests
are run alongside them. Final report names/results are recorded in the delivery.
The Windows engine emits a root-certificate-store warning at startup. Some broad
resource/UI tests retain shutdown resource warnings; assertions and script-error
logs are checked independently instead of treating exit status alone as success.
